# lock21.info SEO audit

> The record of the SEO audit of 2026-10-26 (TODO #89–#91). Method: the `seo-audit` and `schema` skills
> (`.claude/skills/`), with `natural-writing` for the texts it proposes and `information-architecture-audit` for links.
> No Search Console data was available: everything below comes from the site itself, Lighthouse and public searches.

**Date**: 2026-10-26. **Sources**:

- the build of `main` (`dist/`, 56 pages, crawled for titles, descriptions, canonicals, hreflang, headings, images,
  internal links);
- the live site (v0.16 era, before the page renames: `curl`, Lighthouse 12.8.2 on mobile for `/`, `/modele` and the
  dive log; PageSpeed Insights' API was over its daily quota);
- web searches by an agent (not Google itself: no positions or volumes were measured).

Each finding is marked **fact** (seen) or **inference**.

## Summary

Technically the site is clean, and the problems are in what the pages say to a search engine.

**What already works (fact):**

- a self-referencing canonical on every page;
- hreflang `fr` / `en` / `x-default`, reciprocal, on all 56 pages;
- one H1 per page, every image with an alt text, no broken internal link;
- `robots.txt` and the sitemap are sound;
- each of the 27 live addresses that `main` renames has a 301 to its new address (no chain, every target exists);
- Lighthouse: SEO 100 everywhere, performance 99–100 except `/modele` (69, the 3D viewer).

The home page is indexed: a search for « Écluse 21 » with photogrammetry or 3D finds it first (fact).

**The five that matter most**

1. **S1**: 38 of 56 pages share one generic description, and the English pages carry it in French.
2. **S4**: no page answers what people ask about the place (how deep, how strong the current, where to go in). The
   answers that rank today mix 60 ft, 65 ft / 20 m and « a little over 18 m », and none is a measured profile.
3. **S2**: titles say little: « Écluse 21 | Écluse 21 » on the home page, and no title names Long Sault, the dive or
   the St. Lawrence.
4. **S3**: the deploy of `main` renames 27 indexed addresses (the 301s are ready); the Docusaurus addresses of
   2024–2025 (`/blog`, its feeds, `/docs/…`) return a bare 404.
5. **S5**: Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools are, as far as the repo shows, not set up, and the GitHub README
   still says the site is not indexed.

### S1 · Descriptions (fact)

- **What happens:**
  - 38 pages use Starlight's site-wide description, « Photogrammétrie sous-marine de l'écluse 21 (Dickinson
    Landing): carnet de terrain et modèle 3D », in French on the English pages too.
  - The search engine shows it, or makes up its own snippet.
  - The 18 pages with their own `description` are fine (60–155 characters).
- **Fix:**
  - a `description` in a page's frontmatter, in its language, about 120–155 characters, saying what the page
    answers (this was also usability V32);
  - for the pages without one: no description at all rather than a generic one (Christian's decision). The search
    engine then builds the snippet from the page's own text. Lighthouse flags a missing description; that costs
    a point on its score, not in the results.

### S2 · Titles (fact; effect inference)

- **What happens:**
  - The title format is `<page title> | Écluse 21` (`| Lock 21` in English).
  - The home page reads « Écluse 21 | Écluse 21 » and « Lock 21 | Lock 21 ».
  - `/modele` is « Modèle 3D | Écluse 21 ».
  - No title has « Long Sault », « plongée » / “dive” or « Saint-Laurent ». English searchers say “Lock 21” and so do
    many French ones; French titles never say it.
- **Fix** (wording for Christian):
  - home: « Écluse 21 (Lock 21), Long Sault : plongée et modèle 3D » / “Lock 21 dive site, Long Sault: 3D model and
    field notes”;
  - `/modele`: « Modèle 3D de l'écluse 21 » (its H1 already says so).
  - Notebook pages can keep short titles; the site name after them gives the context.

### S3 · Addresses that change or died (fact)

- **The deploy of `main`:**
  - The live site still serves the old addresses (`/questions/…`, `/workflow/…`, `/en/workflow/…`): 50 URLs in its
    sitemap.
  - `main` renames 27 of them. `public/_redirects` and the build's English redirects cover all 27 with a 301 straight
    to the new page.
  - Deploying soon lets Google move its index while few pages are known. After the deploy, submit the sitemap (S5).
- **Docusaurus addresses (2024–2025):**
  - The Wayback Machine holds `/blog`, `/blog/rss.xml`, `/blog/atom.xml`; `/docs/…` paths 404 too.
  - None appear in search results today.
  - A few cheap 301s (`/blog*` → `/`, `/docs/*` → `/:splat`) cost nothing.

### S4 · The answer page (inference, from what ranks)

- **What ranks today:**
  - dive shops (DECOSTOP, O2ToGo), SSI's dive guide, Narcity quoting Parks of the St. Lawrence, Shotline Diving,
    David Gibbins, ScubaBoard, YouTube;
  - in French, Scubarama's « Écluse 21 » page.
- **Where they disagree:** depth (60 ft, 65 ft / 20 m, « a little over 18 m ») and current (2–3 knots).
- **What lock21.info has that none of them has:** 47 dives, a measured bottom (60 ft / 18.3 m), zone depths and a 3D
  model.
- **What's missing:** no page says it in its first two sentences.
- **Fix:** « À propos de l'écluse 21 » / “About Lock 21” could open with where it is, how deep, and the current,
  then the history. Entry (Macdonell Island, the buoy, the rope) is the one topic the site doesn't cover; whether to
  add it is Christian's call.
  - Short sentences stating measured facts are also what AI answers quote. For « how deep is Lock 21 », the answer
    found no depth at all.

### S10 · `http://` in the index (fact)

- **What happens:** the home page was found as `http://lock21.info/`, and GitHub's About link is `http`.
- **Why it's minor:** `http` redirects to `https` (301) and the canonical is `https`, so this fixes itself.
- **Fix:** update the About link.

### S11 · The workers.dev address (fact)

- **What happens:** `lock21-info.lock21.workers.dev` serves every page with a 200. The canonicals point to
  `lock21.info`, so a duplicate is unlikely.
- **Fix:** turn off the workers.dev route (`workers_dev: false` in `wrangler.jsonc`), unless it is used for previews.

### S12 · Trailing slash redirect (fact)

- **What happens:** `/modele/` answers 307 (Cloudflare's `drop-trailing-slash`). Search engines treat a 307 as
  temporary.
- **Why it's minor:** internal links never use the slash, and canonicals say `/modele`.

### S13 · Heading levels (fact)

« Netteté des images » and “Image sharpness” jump from the title (H1) to an H3.

### S14 · Thin pages (fact)

- **Under 150 words:**
  - « La calibration des couleurs » (95 words, EN 82);
  - « Remerciements » (144);
  - « Alignement de chunks » in English (143);
  - the tests, out of the sitemap since usability V9.
- **What to do:** nothing urgent; filled placeholders (usability V6) will grow them.

### S15 · `/modele` weight (fact)

- **Lighthouse on mobile:**
  - LCP 1.7 s and CLS 0.01, both good;
  - total blocking time 4.5 s and 54 MB of tiles, which is the 3D viewer doing its job.
- **Compression, measured after the audit:** Lighthouse's « 2.6 MB to save » is misleading. The zone tiles are
  already compressed inside (KTX2 textures, meshopt geometry): gzip saves 1.8 % on a 5 MB tile. The gain is in four
  files that aren't:
  - `context.glb` (3.0 MB, 41 % with gzip): the terrain and context, already in meshopt, whose output is meant to be
    compressed again by gzip or Zstandard;
  - `mockups.glb` (1.1 MB, 19 %), not in meshopt;
  - `sky.hdr` (1.4 MB, 33 %) and `crayfish.glb` (0.9 MB, 26 %), on the site.
  - Fix: meshopt for the context and the mockups in the publishing pipeline (more than gzip would give), and a
    Cloudflare compression rule (dashboard › Rules › Compression) for `model/gltf-binary` and `.hdr`.
- **Console errors:** the 503s on the home page come from Cloudflare's own prefetch (Speed Brain) and are harmless.

### S16 · Small markup details (fact)

- `og:locale` should be `fr_CA` / `en_CA`.
- The 404 pages declare a canonical and hreflang. Their status is 404, so search engines ignore them; removing them is
  tidy, not needed.
