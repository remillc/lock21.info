title: About Lock 21
description: Lock 21 of the Cornwall Canal in a few points, from its building to its flooding in 1958, and links to learn more.
---

Lock 21 lies under 60 feet (18.3 m) of water in the St. Lawrence, near Long Sault, Ontario. The tops of the piers are
at 40 feet (12 m). The current is strong: in the lock I [measured](/en/problemes/fort-courant-canal) about 35 feet
per minute (10.7 m/min) in June 2024; it is stronger over the spillway.

<p class="fact"><span>Depth</span><b>60 ft (18.3 m)</b></p>
<p class="fact"><span>Tops of the piers</span><b>40 ft (12 m)</b></p>
<p class="fact"><span>Chamber</span><b>270 × 45 ft</b></p>
<p class="fact"><span>Rebuilt</span><b>1883–1886</b></p>
<p class="fact"><span>Flooded</span><b>July 1, 1958</b></p>
</div>

Lock 21 was the last lock of the Cornwall Canal, at its upstream head, at the village of Dickinson's Landing. The canal,
opened in the early 1840s, bypassed the Long Sault rapids from Cornwall to Dickinson's Landing, over about 11 miles.

It was a guard lock: no difference in level between upstream and downstream. The one we dive today dates from the
canal's enlargement, rebuilt from 1883 to 1886: a chamber 270 feet by 45 (82 m by 14 m), and a spillway at its upstream
end.

On July 1, 1958, the Robert H. Saunders dam, built for the St. Lawrence Seaway, flooded the lock, Dickinson's Landing
and other villages along the shore: the Lost Villages. Their people had been moved to Long Sault and Ingleside.

In 1988 and 1989, Nick Baets and the Ottawa chapter of Save Ontario Shipwrecks dived the lock and drew it. His 1988
drawing is the basis of the [site zones](/en/decoupage-site).
